01 / Briefing / Flight rules

Six habits that keep you out of trouble.

Writing, studying, coding, it applies to all of it. One catch: your school’s AI policy comes first, and it might be stricter than anything here.

  1. Be honest

    Say when you used AI and how, if your teacher asks. Handing in AI work as your own is cheating, even if nobody catches it.

  2. Think first

    Try the problem yourself before you ask. Use AI to explain, quiz, or give feedback so you build skills instead of skipping them.

  3. Check everything

    AI can state wrong facts with total confidence and make up sources. Verify anything important in a textbook or trusted site.

  4. Guard your privacy

    Don’t type in passwords, addresses, or other people’s personal info. Assume anything you enter could be stored.

  5. Watch for bias

    AI learns from human data and can repeat stereotypes or leave people out. Ask whether an answer is fair and whose view is missing.

  6. Follow the rules

    Rules change from class to class. Not sure if AI is allowed on an assignment? Ask your teacher first.

What’s fine

Asking AI to explain a concept in simpler words, then summarizing it yourself.
Having AI quiz you before a test.
Asking for feedback on your own draft, then revising it yourself.
Brainstorming topic ideas, then researching your pick.

What’s not

Submitting AI-written work as your own.
Using AI during a test or quiz when it is not allowed.
Citing sources the AI gave you without checking they exist.
Making fake images or messages about real people.
